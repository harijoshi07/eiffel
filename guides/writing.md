## 1. Cheat sheet

### Both written tasks
1. Do exactly what the bullets or question ask. Read everything first (Lesson 3 p.12, Lesson 5 p.20).
2. Write complete sentences with correct vocabulary and grammar.
3. **Elaborate.** Add a reason, detail or example. A bare statement is not enough.
4. Use your own words. A response "mostly borrowed from the stimulus" scores 1.
5. A word counter exists. **Spell-check does not** (Lesson 1, p.6).

### Email: 7 minutes
- One clear answer to each bullet, in the order given.
- Polite, appropriate tone.
- A clear request or action.

### Discussion: 10 minutes, at least 100 words
1. State your opinion.
2. Explain why, with details and examples.
3. Move the discussion on: add a new perspective or ask your own question (Lesson 4, p.17).

Use what the other students said.

### Build a Sentence
Every item is a grammar problem. Section 4 has the rules ETS teaches.

## 4. Build a Sentence toolkit (Lessons 1-2)

ETS says this task tests sentence structure, which "is essential for all written communication" (Lesson 1, p.3).

### Clause terms (Lesson 2, p.7)
- **Independent clause:** subject + verb, can stand alone.
- **Dependent clause:** subject + verb, cannot stand alone.
- **Phrase:** missing a subject or a verb.

| Type | ETS examples (key, p.11) |
|---|---|
| Independent | "do you know the time"; "moreover, she forgot to bring the report" |
| Dependent | "even though the weather was nice"; "unless they lower the price"; "until the meeting ended" |
| Phrase | "in spite of the cold"; "by the river"; "approximately 50 years ago" |

### Connectors (Lesson 2, p.11)

**Coordinating conjunctions:** and, for, nor, but, or, yet, so
- Comma before them.
- *I couldn't find my phone this morning, so I was late for class.*

**Subordinating conjunctions:** because, although, even though, since, unless, while
- No comma if the dependent clause comes second.
- Comma if it comes first.
- *Because I preferred reading, I didn't like sports.*

**Conjunctive adverbs:** however, furthermore, moreover, nevertheless, therefore
- Comma after it at the start of a sentence.
- Can follow a semicolon.
- *I took the bus; however, the train would have been faster.*

### How ETS explains an answer (Lesson 1, p.1)
Say **why** a word goes where it goes. Example: "who" follows "know" because "know" needs an object, and "who" connects the verb to the clause after it. Explain every placement this way.

### [data] What the 70 items look like
- 44 answers are questions, 26 are statements.
- Tests 3 and 4 are all statements (reported speech, negation).
- 27 items have one spare tile. 43 use every tile.

### [mine] Method for each item
1. Read the line they say.
2. Place the printed words first. They fix the opening, the ending and the punctuation.
3. Question or statement? That decides where subject and verb go.
4. Embedded clause (tell me, know, wonder, wanted to know, whether, if)? Inside it, use **subject then verb**.
5. If a tile fits nowhere, it may be the spare.
6. For each leftover chunk, ask: **which word does this belong to?**
7. Read the whole sentence once.

## 5. Write an Email: playbook

### ETS guidance (Lesson 3, p.12; Lesson 1, p.3)
- Read the scenario carefully.
- Answer the assigned question.
- Write complete sentences, with correct vocabulary and grammar.
- The three bullets are the content you must cover.
- You share information "for a specific communicative purpose".

### [mine] A 7-minute shape

| Time | Do |
|---|---|
| ~1 min | Read. Jot one detail per bullet. |
| ~5 min | Write. |
| ~1 min | Check word forms, agreement, and that every bullet has a detail. |

**Order of the email**
1. Greeting
2. Why you are writing
3. One part per bullet, in the order given
4. A closing line with the action you want
5. Sign-off

The 3-to-4 gap is elaboration, so give each bullet more than one bare statement.

## 6. Academic Discussion: playbook

### ETS guidance
- **Three actions** (Lesson 4, p.17):
  1. State your opinion, recommendation or thought.
  2. Explain why, with details and examples.
  3. Add a new perspective or your own question.
- A good contribution "involves more than simply stating your opinion" (Lesson 4, p.15).
- Read the professor's post and the student posts before you write (Lesson 5, p.20).
- Answer the assigned question in complete sentences.
- The task says: "Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words" (Lesson 1, p.4).

### ETS criteria for a response (Lesson 5, p.21)
- Clearly elaborates an argument for a position.
- Responds to the other posts or uses their information.
- Is adequately supported, clear and cohesive.
- Uses an accurate range of grammar and vocabulary.
- Follows spelling, punctuation and capitalisation conventions.

### [mine] A 10-minute shape

| Time | Do |
|---|---|
| ~1.5 min | Read the three posts. Pick your side and one student to answer. |
| ~7 min | Write 100+ words. |
| ~1.5 min | Check. |

**Order of the post**
1. Your position, tied to one student's point
2. Your reason
3. An example
4. Something new: a second reason or a question

Repeating what a student already said adds nothing new.

## 7. The two official samples

ETS calls both "high-level" (Lessons 3 and 5) but **gives no score for either**. Do not assume they are 5s.

In the texts below, **bold with a number** marks an error. The fixes are listed after each text and are mine, not ETS's.

### 7a. Email sample (Lesson 3, p.14): 118 words

> Hi Editor,
>
> I hope you are well! I wanted to say that I really love the new Sunshine Poetry Magazine. It's refreshing to see such a diverse range of voices, and the layout is very **engageing¹**.
>
> However, I had some trouble with **online submission form²** when I tried to submit two of my poems. After I uploaded the files and hit submit, the page just kept loading and loading, and eventually, I wasn't sure if **my poems can sucessfully sent³**.
>
> Could you please confirm if you **recieved⁴** my submissions?
>
> I'm really excited to be a part of your magazine and would appreciate any help you can provide to fix this issue. Looking forward to your response.
>
> Best regards.

*Paragraph breaks above are added for readability. The ETS text is one block.*

**What each part does**

| Sentences | Job |
|---|---|
| "Hi Editor, I hope you are well!" | Greeting, polite opener |
| "I wanted to say… the layout is very engageing." | **Bullet 1:** what it likes. Two specifics: the voices and the layout |
| "However, I had some trouble… my poems can sucessfully sent." | **Bullet 2:** the problem, step by step (uploaded, hit submit, page kept loading) |
| "Could you please confirm…?" | **Bullet 3:** a direct, polite request |
| "I'm really excited… Looking forward to your response." | Warm close and an offer to wait for the reply |

**What to copy**
- It follows the bullets in order.
- Every bullet has its own detail.
- The problem is told as a sequence, so the reader can picture it.
- "However" and "After I uploaded…" give variety. The request starts with "Could you please".

**Errors (my fixes)**

| # | In the sample | Fix | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | engageing | engaging | Spelling (typo) |
| 2 | with online submission form | with **the** online submission form | Missing article |
| 3 | my poems can sucessfully sent | my poems **had been successfully sent** | Grammar (tense and passive) |
| 4 | recieved | received | Spelling (typo) |

> **Takeaway.** Items 1, 4 (and "sucessfully") are typos, which the rubric tolerates at 5. Items 2 and 3 are grammar errors, and the 5 row says "almost no errors". ETS still calls the sample high-level, but do not copy its grammar.

### 7b. Discussion sample (Lesson 5, p.22): 79 words

> While I get **Andrew's** point about limited free time, I think it's good to have mandatory volunteer hours at high schools. Volunteering teaches students empathy and community service, helping them grow into responsible adults. Schools can give flexible options that fit **student's¹** schedules, like weekend or summer volunteering. What's more, volunteering can be a break from academic pressure, allowing students to engage in meaningful activities outside school. For example, helping at a **comunity²** garden is both fulfilling and educational.

**What each sentence does**

| # | Sentence starts | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "While I get Andrew's point…" | Names a student, concedes, then **states the position** |
| 2 | "Volunteering teaches students…" | **Reason 1** |
| 3 | "Schools can give flexible options…" | **Answers Andrew's objection** (limited free time) |
| 4 | "What's more, volunteering can be a break…" | **Reason 2**, which is new |
| 5 | "For example, helping at a comunity garden…" | **One concrete example** |

**What to copy**
- It opens by naming a student and conceding a point.
- It then answers that student's objection, so it uses the other posts.
- Every sentence has a job. Nothing repeats.
- The connectors are *while*, *what's more* and *for example*.

**Errors (my fixes)**

| # | In the sample | Fix | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | student's schedules | **students'** schedules | Possessive (grammar) |
| 2 | comunity | community | Spelling (typo) |

> **Takeaway.** The sample has **79 words, below ETS's own 100-word minimum**. Do not use it as a length target. Aim for 100 or more.

## Patterns from my log

Build a Sentence: **65/69**. Tests 1-4 were 40/40. Tests 5-7 were 25/29. Exact sentences are in `logs/writing.md`.

- **Every miss was an attachment error:** a chunk placed next to the wrong neighbour. For each leftover tile ask, "which word does this belong to?"
- **Embedded question order (TR5 Q6).** After "tell me / know / wonder", use subject then verb. You got 25 of 26 such items right, so this is one slip, not a weakness.
- **"any of" + plural group (TR3 Q8).** Build the skeleton first, then ask where "any of" can go.
- **Same-subject "expect" (TR3 Q7).** When "you expect" and "you complete" share a subject, use "expect to complete", with no noun between them.
- **Arguable item (TR4 Q1).** Both placements of "last month" are grammatical. No method settles it. Do not change your approach for it.
- **"Sounds fine" is not the test.** Three of the four wrong sentences read smoothly. The test is whether the sentence fits the printed frame and the context line.
