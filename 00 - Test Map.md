# Test Map — TOEFL iBT (January 21, 2026 format)

## Section overview

| Section | Items | Base time | Task types |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Reading | 50 | 30 min | Complete the Words · Read in Daily Life · Read an Academic Passage |
| Listening | 47 | 29 min | Listen and Choose a Response · Conversation · Announcement · Academic Talk |
| Writing | 12 | 23 min | Build a Sentence · Write an Email · Academic Discussion |
| Speaking | 11 | 8 min | Listen and Repeat · Take an Interview |

Total seated time is approximately **90 minutes of active work** plus check-in and instructions — expect around **2 hours at the centre**.

---

## Skeleton at a glance

**Order:** Reading → Listening → Writing → Speaking. About 2 hours in total, including volume check and instructions. Unscored **pretest** items are mixed into Reading or Listening, so you cannot tell which items count.

### Can I go back?

| Where | Go back? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reading, inside a module | **Yes.** Next and Back work within the module. A clock shows the time left for the module. | Practice test PDFs, Reading Module 1 and 2 pages |
| Reading, Module 1 → Module 2 | **No.** You cannot return to Module 1 once Module 2 starts. | Same pages; Teacher FAQ Q9 |
| Listening | **No, never.** Next only. A clock shows the time for each question. | Practice test PDFs, Listening pages |
| Writing | Not stated in the official documents I read. | Gap |
| Speaking | Each task is one-way. In Listen and Repeat you repeat each sentence **once**. No preparation time in either task. | Overview PDF; practice test p.34-35 |

Adaptivity: Reading and Listening each have two stages (modules). Your score on stage 1 sets the difficulty of stage 2. You cannot skip or go back between stages (Teacher FAQ Q8-9).

### Items per task

Counts for the real test are "up to" numbers and can change as the test adapts (Teacher FAQ Q1). The **practice forms** (Full-Length 1-2, Teacher Resources 1-5) are shorter: 40 Reading items and 34 Listening items. Use the practice counts for pacing practice only.

**Reading** (real: up to 50 items, base 30 min; practice: 2 modules × 20 = 40)

| Task | Per module in practice | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Complete the Words | Q1-10 | One text of about 70-100 words. After an intact first sentence, the second half of every second word is missing. 10 blanks. |
| Read in Daily Life | Q11-15 | Two short texts (notice, email, post, schedule, text messages), 15-150 words each, with 2-3 questions each. |
| Read an Academic Passage | Q16-20 | One passage of about 200 words, 5 questions: detail, vocabulary in context, inference, relationship between ideas, purpose. |

**Listening** (real: up to 47 items, base 29 min; practice: Module 1 = 18, Module 2 = 16)

| Task | Per module in practice | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Listen and Choose a Response | Q1-8, both modules | You hear a question or statement. It is **not printed**. You read four options and pick the best reply. |
| Listen to a Conversation | 2 questions per conversation. Module 1: two conversations (Q9-12). Module 2: one (Q9-10). | Two speakers, campus or everyday topics. |
| Listen to an Announcement | 2 questions (Module 1 Q13-14, Module 2 Q11-12) | About 40-85 words: schedules, rules, events. |
| Listen to an Academic Talk | 4 questions (Module 1 Q15-18, Module 2 Q13-16) | About 100-250 words, no background knowledge needed. |

Accents: North American, British and Australian.

**Writing** (real: up to 12 items, base 23 min; practice: 12)

| Task | Items | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Build a Sentence | 10. Put the given words and phrases in order to reply to a prompt sentence. | A clock runs; the length is not stated. By subtraction it is about 6 min of the 23 (my arithmetic, not official). |
| Write an Email | 1 | 7 min |
| Write for an Academic Discussion | 1. An effective response is at least 100 words. | 10 min |

**Speaking** (real: up to 11 items, base 8 min; practice: 11)

| Task | Items | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Listen and Repeat | 7 sentences in one scenario, getting longer | 8-12 s to speak after each sentence. Heard once. No preparation. |
| Take an Interview | 4 questions from a recorded interviewer | 45 s per answer. No preparation. |

### What is official and what is my assumption
- Official: section table, task names, text lengths, item counts per task in the practice forms, the Back/Next rules above, order of sections, adaptive design.
- Not stated in what I read: whether Writing allows going back, how long each Listening question clock runs, and how many items in Reading and Listening are unscored pretest.
- My own design, not ETS: the "play once, options after the audio" behaviour of `practice/listen-and-respond.html` and `practice/listen-to-conversation.html`. The official text says Listening has no return and that Type A prompts are not printed. It says nothing about replaying audio, so treat replay as unavailable.
- Sources: ets.org content page (section table, checked online); Teacher FAQ and Test Overview PDFs (ETS); the instruction pages of `resources/pdfs/full-length-1.pdf`.

## Scoring

| What | Detail |
|---|---|
| Scale | **1 – 6** per section, reported in **0.5-point increments** |
| Overall | Average of four section scores, rounded to nearest half band |
| Transition score | ETS also reports a comparable 0–120 score during the transition period |

### Band meanings

| Band | CEFR | What it signals |
|---:|---|---|
| 6 | C2 | Near-native; virtually no errors; full nuance |
| 5 – 5.5 | C1 | Strong command; occasional minor slips; clear organisation |
| 4 – 4.5 | B2 | Good functional ability; some errors; handles most tasks |
| 3 – 3.5 | B1 | Basic competence; simplified language; frequent errors |
| 2 – 2.5 | A2 | Very limited; handles only simple prompts |
| 1 – 1.5 | A1 | Minimal ability; most language is inaccessible |

> **Do not convert raw practice accuracy into a band score directly.** Reading and Listening are multistage adaptive — your score depends on item difficulty, not a simple percentage.

---

## Adaptivity rules

Reading and Listening adjust difficulty based on your performance. This means:

- Treat every item as consequential from the first.
- Do not rush early modules to "save time" — it backfires.
- Harder material in a later module is often a sign of strong early performance.
- You cannot return to an earlier module once it closes.

## What each section actually measures

### Reading
- Spelling reconstruction from partial letters
- Practical comprehension of notices, emails, schedules, and digital texts
- Academic main ideas, detail, inference, vocabulary, and author purpose

### Listening
- Socially appropriate short replies
- Purpose, attitude, implication, and next action in conversations
- Key details and organisation in announcements
- Concepts, contrasts, examples, and speaker purpose in academic talks

### Writing
- Word order and grammatical sentence assembly
- Clear, functional email communication
- A concise, supported contribution to an academic discussion

### Speaking
- Accurate reproduction of spoken sentences
- Intelligible pronunciation, rhythm, and phrasing
- Direct, developed answers to interview questions

## What is retired (do not train with these as format simulations)

| Retired feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Two long 700-word Reading passages as the full section | Current section is shorter and adaptive |
| Three lectures + two campus conversations as full Listening | Current section has different task types |
| Integrated Writing (read a passage, listen to a lecture, write) | Replaced by Write an Email + Academic Discussion |
| Four Speaking tasks (1 independent + 3 integrated) | Replaced by Listen and Repeat + Take an Interview |
| 0–120 total score / 0–30 per-section score | Now 1–6 per section |

Older books and courses can still build vocabulary, grammar, and academic comprehension. Label them as **language drills**, not format practice.

---

## Test-day principles

1. **Accuracy before drama.** Clear, controlled English scores better than forced sophistication.
2. **Answer the exact task.** Relevance is scored.
3. **Use structure.** Signal purpose, reasons, examples, conclusions.
4. **Recover immediately.** One bad item must not affect the next.
5. **Protect energy.** Eat, hydrate, and sleep well before test day.
