# Speaking Playbook

## Mission

Produce intelligible, accurate, appropriately developed speech. The current section contains 11 items across Listen and Repeat and Take an Interview, with an approximate base time of 8 minutes.

## 1. Listen and Repeat

You hear a sentence and repeat it. This is not a memory contest alone: it tests whether you can perceive and reproduce meaningful spoken chunks.

### Listening priorities

Capture the sentence in layers:

1. **Meaning:** What happened?
2. **Skeleton:** Who did what?
3. **Content words:** nouns, main verbs, adjectives, numbers.
4. **Grammar words:** articles, auxiliaries, prepositions, endings.
5. **Delivery:** stress, rhythm, and phrasing.

### Chunking

Hear:

> Students who submitted the form / before Friday / will receive confirmation by email.

Repeat by phrase, not as fifteen isolated words.

### Recovery

If one word disappears:

- keep speaking;
- preserve the sentence’s meaning and structure;
- do not restart repeatedly;
- finish with controlled delivery.

A brief omission is less damaging than a long breakdown.

### Daily drill

1. Play one sentence only once.
2. Wait a moment without writing it.
3. Repeat and record.
4. Compare against the source.
5. Mark errors:
   - omitted content;
   - changed grammar;
   - wrong word;
   - unclear sound;
   - broken rhythm.
6. Repeat once correctly, then move on.

### Pronunciation priorities

- finish plural and past-tense endings;
- stress content words;
- reduce function words naturally;
- pause at phrase boundaries, not inside noun or verb phrases;
- favor clarity over imitating an accent.

## 2. Take an Interview

Answer questions from a virtual interviewer. Strong responses are direct, relevant, developed, and natural.

### PREP structure

```text
P — Point: answer immediately
R — Reason: explain why
E — Example: make it concrete
P — Point: close or state the result
```

Example:

> I prefer studying in the morning because I can concentrate before messages and errands interrupt me. For example, when I prepared for an economics exam, I reviewed the hardest material from seven to nine each morning and retained much more. So for demanding work, mornings are usually most effective for me.

### Common interview functions

**Preference**

> I prefer X mainly because...

**Past experience**

> One experience that stands out was... It taught me...

**Advice**

> I would recommend... because... A practical first step would be...

**Hypothetical**

> If I were in that situation, I would... The main reason is...

**Agree/disagree**

> I mostly agree, although... For instance...

### Development ladder

If an answer feels too short, add one rung:

1. answer;
2. reason;
3. example;
4. consequence;
5. brief contrast.

Do not add unrelated ideas merely to continue speaking.

## Delivery

- Begin promptly; avoid a long `um`.
- Use sentences you can control.
- Vary emphasis naturally.
- Correct a mistake once and continue.
- End the thought instead of trailing off.

## Self-review

Score each recording 0–2:

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intelligibility | often unclear | mostly understandable | consistently clear |
| Fluency | frequent breakdowns | some hesitation | steady phrasing |
| Language control | errors obscure meaning | errors but meaning clear | accurate and flexible |
| Relevance | misses question | generally relevant | direct and focused |
| Development | fragment/minimal | some support | reason and specific support |

For Listen and Repeat, replace relevance and development with:

- **content accuracy**
- **grammatical accuracy**

## Seven-day micro-routine

Complete this even on non-Speaking days:

- 5 Listen and Repeat items;
- 3 interview answers;
- 1 minute reviewing the recording;
- 1 corrected second attempt.

Ten focused minutes daily is more useful than one long silent study session.
