# Writing Playbook

## Mission

Write clear, relevant, controlled English under time pressure. The section contains 12 items across three task types and takes approximately 23 minutes.

## 1. Build a Sentence

Arrange the supplied words or phrases into a grammatical sentence or question. Some items may include material that does not belong.

### Construction order

1. Find the main subject and finite verb.
2. Decide whether the result is a statement, direct question, or embedded question.
3. Place objects and complements.
4. Attach modifiers next to what they describe.
5. Check tense, agreement, articles, and punctuation.
6. Remove any distractor that duplicates a grammatical role.

### Essential patterns

**Direct question**

`Question word + auxiliary + subject + base verb`

> Where **can she buy** a copy?

**Embedded question**

`introductory clause + question word + subject + verb`

> She wants to know where **she can buy** a copy.

**Relative clause**

> The guide **who showed us the city** was excellent.

**Passive**

> The study guide **was provided by the professor**.

### Trap checklist

- [ ] Did I invert a direct question correctly?
- [ ] Did I avoid inversion inside an embedded question?
- [ ] Does every verb have a subject?
- [ ] Is a distractor creating a duplicate subject or verb?
- [ ] Is the modifier beside the right noun?

## 2. Write an Email

Write for an academic or social situation. The prompt may require a request, explanation, information, or solution.

### Plan by function

Underline each required action in the prompt. Convert it into one content block:

```text
Subject or greeting
Purpose
Required point 1
Required point 2
Request / proposed next step
Thanks and sign-off
```

### Flexible model

> Dear [Name/Title],  
>  
> I am writing because [clear purpose]. [Explain the relevant situation with specific details.] Unfortunately, [describe the problem or constraint]. Could you please [specific request]? If that is not possible, I would appreciate [reasonable alternative].  
>  
> Thank you for your time and assistance.  
> Best regards,  
> [Name]

Use the model as a function map, not a script. Match the relationship: an instructor or administrator usually requires a more formal tone than a friend.

### Seven-minute workflow

- **0:00–1:00:** identify audience, purpose, and every required point;
- **1:00–5:30:** write;
- **5:30–7:00:** check completeness, clarity, verbs, and tone.

There is no benefit in chasing a large word count. A practical training target is roughly **80–120 words**, but complete task fulfillment and language control matter more than hitting a number.

### Email checklist

- [ ] Purpose appears immediately.
- [ ] Every prompt requirement is answered.
- [ ] The request is specific.
- [ ] Tone fits the recipient.
- [ ] Paragraphs are easy to scan.
- [ ] Greeting and sign-off are appropriate.

## 3. Write for an Academic Discussion

Contribute to an online discussion by giving and supporting an opinion. Engage with the professor’s question and, when useful, another student’s idea.

### Reliable structure

1. **Position:** answer the question directly.
2. **Reason:** explain the main logic.
3. **Example:** provide a concrete case.
4. **Connection:** extend or qualify another viewpoint.
5. **Implication:** state why the idea matters.

### Flexible model

> I believe [position] because [main reason]. For example, [specific illustration]. This supports [classmate]’s point that [brief connection], but I would add that [new contribution or qualification]. Therefore, [clear implication].

Do not spend half the response summarizing other students. Your own contribution must be visible.

### Ten-minute workflow

- **0:00–1:30:** choose a position, reason, and example;
- **1:30–8:30:** write;
- **8:30–10:00:** remove repetition and correct obvious errors.

A practical training target is approximately **100–150 words**. Quality and development matter more than inflated length.

## Self-scoring

Give each category 0–2:

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task fulfillment | major parts missing | mostly complete | fully complete |
| Organization | difficult to follow | generally clear | controlled and cohesive |
| Development | unsupported | some support | specific, relevant support |
| Grammar | errors block meaning | noticeable but understandable | strong control |
| Vocabulary | often inaccurate | adequate | precise and natural |

Score out of 10. Rewrite anything below 8.

## High-value editing order

1. Missing prompt point
2. Unclear sentence
3. Wrong verb or word order
4. Repetition
5. Minor spelling or punctuation
