# Listening Playbook

## Mission

Understand what speakers are doing, not merely what words they say. Listening is multistage adaptive; answer each item from the audio evidence without trying to guess your route through the test.

## 1. Listen and Choose a Response

Select the socially and logically appropriate reply.

### Three checks

1. **Speech act:** Is the speaker asking, inviting, apologizing, suggesting, confirming, or complaining?
2. **Grammar:** Does the response fit the question form?
3. **Situation:** Would a real person naturally say it next?

### Frequent traps

- same-topic words but no logical response;
- answering `where` with a time or `when` with a location;
- treating a negative question as a simple vocabulary exercise;
- ignoring implied meaning or polite refusal;
- a grammatical response that is socially strange.

### Quick drill

“Could you send me the revised schedule?”

A. The meeting room is upstairs.  
B. Sure, I’ll email it this afternoon.  
C. No, the schedule was revised.  
D. I attended last week.

Answer: **B**. It accepts the request and states the action.

## 2. Listen to a Conversation

Track:

- why the conversation began;
- each speaker’s problem or goal;
- what changes during the exchange;
- the proposed solution or next action;
- attitude expressed through tone and wording.

### Minimal notes

```text
goal:
problem:
change:
next:
```

### Implication questions

Translate idioms into their conversational function:

- “That slipped my mind.” → I forgot.
- “I’m not sure that’ll work.” → polite disagreement.
- “You can say that again.” → strong agreement.
- “I wouldn’t count on it.” → it is unlikely.

Do not interpret an idiom literally when the context clearly signals attitude.

---

## 3. Listen to an Announcement

Announcements are organized around action.

Capture:

```text
topic:
audience:
change/event:
date/place:
required action:
reason:
```

Pay special attention to:

- corrections: “not Tuesday, but Wednesday”;
- restrictions: “only students who registered”;
- deadlines and exceptions;
- why a detail is mentioned;
- what listeners should do next.

---

## 4. Listen to an Academic Talk

### Structure before detail

Listen for:

- central topic;
- two or three main claims;
- examples attached to each claim;
- contrast between theories, stages, species, periods, or processes;
- cause-and-effect chains;
- the speaker’s reason for mentioning an example.

### Note system

```text
→ causes/results in
≠ contrast
= definition
ex example
+ advantage
- disadvantage
? uncertainty/question
```

Example:

```text
soft attention = effortless + room for thought
hard attention = intense → fatigue
nature ex park → recovery
```

Notes should be readable in seconds. Full sentences waste attention.

## One-listen training

Use current official audio whenever possible.

1. Listen once without pausing or replaying.
2. Answer the questions.
3. Summarize the audio aloud in 20 seconds.
4. Listen again only during review.
5. Compare what you missed with the transcript, if one is provided.

## Error diagnosis

| Error | Fix |
|---|---|
| forgot key detail | note names, numbers, changes, and causes |
| understood words, missed purpose | label the speaker’s goal first |
| chose repeated vocabulary | test the logic of the response |
| lost during example | connect every example to the preceding claim |
| notes became distracting | reduce notes to symbols and keywords |
| missed attitude | listen for stress, hesitation, and correction |

## Final checklist

- [ ] I know why the speaker is talking.
- [ ] I separate the main claim from its example.
- [ ] I notice corrections and changes of plan.
- [ ] I answer from the recording, not outside knowledge.
- [ ] I move on mentally after each item.
