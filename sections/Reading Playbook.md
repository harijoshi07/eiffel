# Reading Playbook

## Mission

Answer quickly without replacing evidence with intuition. Reading is multistage adaptive, so protect accuracy from the first item.

## 1. Complete the Words

You see a passage in which parts of selected words are missing. Restore each word from spelling, grammar, and meaning.

### Four-pass method

1. **Read the whole sentence.** Predict the meaning before filling letters.
2. **Identify the grammar slot.** Is it a noun, verb, adjective, adverb, connector, or function word?
3. **Match the visible letters and length.** Your answer must fit every supplied character.
4. **Reread the completed sentence.** Check agreement, tense, spelling, and meaning.

### High-yield patterns

- articles (`a`, `an`, `the`) predict a noun phrase;
- modal + base verb: `may occur`, not `may occurs`;
- preposition + noun/gerund: `by studying`;
- common suffixes: `-tion`, `-ment`, `-ity`, `-ous`, `-ive`, `-ly`;
- contrast markers: `however`, `although`, `whereas`;
- cause markers: `because`, `therefore`, `consequently`.

### Trap

A word can fit the meaning but fail the visible spelling pattern. Never ignore the letters already supplied.

### Drill

Complete each word:

1. The committee rea___d a decision after a lengthy debate.
2. Alth____ the material is lightweight, it is remarkably strong.
3. The discovery had a signifi____ impact on later research.
4. Plants con____ sunlight into chemical energy.
5. The policy was introduced gra_____ rather than all at once.

Answers: `reached`, `although`, `significant`, `convert`, `gradually`.

## 2. Read in Daily Life

Texts may resemble emails, notices, schedules, posts, webpages, or instructions.

### Read by function

Before reading every detail, identify:

- **sender/source**
- **audience**
- **purpose**
- **required action**
- **deadline/location**
- **exception or condition**

### Evidence map

| Question asks about | Look for |
|---|---|
| main purpose | subject line, opening, final request |
| what to do | imperatives, links, contact details |
| when/where | dates, times, locations |
| who qualifies | restrictions, exceptions, eligibility |
| implication | facts that combine to support one conclusion |

### Elimination rules

Reject an option if it:

- is true but does not answer the question;
- exaggerates `may` into `will` or `some` into `all`;
- reverses who performs an action;
- confuses a deadline with an event date;
- introduces a plausible detail not stated or implied.

### Mini-drill

> Library Study Rooms: Reservations are limited to two hours per group. Rooms left unoccupied for more than 15 minutes may be reassigned. Same-day extensions are possible only when no other group is waiting.

Why should a group avoid leaving a room empty?

A. The library charges an absence fee.  
B. The reservation may be given to another group.  
C. Extensions must be requested 15 minutes early.  
D. Rooms can be reserved only on the same day.

Answer: **B**. “May be reassigned” directly supports it.

## 3. Read an Academic Passage

### Paragraph map

After each paragraph, retain a short label:

- problem
- old explanation
- new evidence
- example
- contrast
- consequence

Do not memorize sentences. Track the argument.

### Question playbook

**Main idea**
- Choose the option broad enough to cover the passage but specific enough to state its point.
- Reject a single-example answer.

**Detail**
- Return to the relevant sentence.
- Read one sentence before and after it.
- Match meaning, not repeated keywords.

**Inference**
- Choose what must or strongly follows from the text.
- Reject answers that are merely possible.

**Vocabulary**
- Substitute each option into the sentence.
- Use local meaning; ignore a common definition that does not fit context.

**Author purpose**
- Ask what the example does: define, illustrate, contrast, challenge, or support.

**Negative factual / EXCEPT**
- Verify three supported options.
- Select the one not supported; do not choose the most surprising true statement.

### Timing

- Move immediately when evidence is decisive.
- If stuck between two options, compare each against the exact wording and scope.
- Do not spend disproportionate time protecting one item while sacrificing several later items.

For every missed question, write:

3. Why the correct option is better:
