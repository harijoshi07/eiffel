## Task 2 — Read in Daily Life

**Rules before every text:**
1. Identify: sender · audience · purpose · required action · deadline · exceptions.
2. Eliminate options that are true but don't answer the question, exaggerate ("some" → "all"), reverse roles, or add unverified details.

### Mini-text 1

> **Notice — Campus Fitness Centre**
> The swimming pool will be closed for maintenance from Monday 9 November through Wednesday 11 November. Scheduled classes will resume Thursday 12 November. Students who booked sessions during the closure may reschedule through the online portal by Friday 7 November. No refunds will be issued for sessions that are not rescheduled.

**Q1.** What must students do to keep their session credit?
- A. Request a refund by 11 November
- B. Contact the fitness centre by telephone
- C. Reschedule through the online portal by 7 November
- D. Attend an alternative class on 12 November

**Q2.** What happens if a student does nothing?
- A. Their session is automatically rescheduled
- B. They receive a partial refund
- C. They lose the credit for their booked session
- D. They are banned from the pool for one week

**Answers:** C · C

## Task 3 — Read an Academic Passage

**Before each passage:**
- Skim: read only the first sentence of each paragraph (30 sec max).
- Label each paragraph: problem / explanation / evidence / example / contrast / consequence.
- Then answer questions from the text, not from memory.

### Passage 1 — Cognitive Load Theory

Learning is most effective when the amount of information processed at one time matches the capacity of working memory. Cognitive load theory, developed by John Sweller in the 1980s, identifies three types of load: intrinsic load, determined by the complexity of the material itself; extraneous load, caused by poor instructional design; and germane load, the mental effort dedicated to forming long-term schemas.

Practitioners applying this theory recommend breaking complex tasks into smaller components before integrating them. A novice learning a musical instrument, for example, benefits from practising hand positions and rhythm separately before combining them. Research consistently shows that reducing extraneous load — by removing irrelevant visuals or redundant text — improves both retention and transfer.

Critics note that germane load is difficult to measure independently and may overlap with intrinsic load. This has led some researchers to propose a simplified two-load model, arguing that the distinction between germane and intrinsic load is not supported by neurological evidence.

**Q1 — Main idea.** What is the primary purpose of this passage?
- A. To compare Sweller's work with competing memory theories
- B. To explain a theory of learning and note a debate about it
- C. To argue that germane load is the most important factor in instruction
- D. To describe the history of cognitive science

**Q2 — Detail.** According to the passage, what does extraneous load result from?
- A. The complexity of the material itself
- B. The effort required to build long-term schemas
- C. Poor instructional design
- D. Neurological limitations in working memory

**Q3 — Inference.** What does the passage imply about a classroom with too many decorative visual elements?
- A. Students will find the content more engaging
- B. Germane load is likely to increase
- C. Extraneous load may harm learning outcomes
- D. Intrinsic load will decrease

**Q4 — Vocabulary.** The word "schemas" (paragraph 1) most closely means:
- A. Short-term impressions
- B. Organised mental frameworks
- C. Written study notes
- D. Visual memory aids

**Q5 — Author purpose.** Why does the author mention a musician learning an instrument?
- A. To argue that music is a uniquely complex skill
- B. To illustrate how breaking tasks into parts reduces cognitive load
- C. To contrast a novice approach with an expert approach
- D. To support the critics' argument about germane load

**Answers:** B · C · C · B · B

### Passage 2 — The Columbian Exchange

When European explorers reached the Americas in the late fifteenth century, they initiated one of history's largest biological events: the transfer of plants, animals, and diseases between hemispheres that had been isolated for millennia. Historians call this process the Columbian Exchange.

The Americas provided crops — maize, potatoes, tomatoes, and cacao — that transformed European and Asian diets and contributed to population growth in those regions. Europe, in turn, introduced wheat, cattle, horses, and pigs to the Americas. The horse, in particular, dramatically altered the cultures of many indigenous groups, enabling more efficient hunting and widening territorial control.

The most devastating element of the exchange, however, was biological. Indigenous populations had no immunity to European diseases such as smallpox, measles, and influenza. Mortality estimates for the Americas in the century following first contact range from 50 to 90 percent in affected communities, making it one of the worst demographic catastrophes in recorded history.

**Q1 — Main idea.**
- A. To argue that European colonisation was primarily beneficial
- B. To describe the biological and cultural consequences of contact between hemispheres
- C. To focus on the role of the horse in indigenous cultures
- D. To explain why indigenous populations lacked immunity to disease

**Q2 — Detail.** What effect did the potato have according to the passage?
- A. It led to the domestication of cattle in Europe
- B. It contributed to population growth in Europe and Asia
- C. It replaced maize as the primary crop of the Americas
- D. It reduced mortality from disease in affected regions

**Q3 — Inference.** What does the passage imply about European agriculture before the Columbian Exchange?
- A. It was more advanced than agriculture in the Americas
- B. It did not include potatoes, tomatoes, or maize
- C. It relied primarily on animal husbandry rather than crops
- D. It was already capable of feeding a global population

**Q4 — Negative factual (EXCEPT).** All of the following were transferred FROM the Americas EXCEPT:
- A. Maize
- B. Cacao
- C. Wheat
- D. Tomatoes

**Q5 — Vocabulary.** "Demographic" (paragraph 3) most closely relates to:
- A. Geographic boundaries
- B. Cultural traditions
- C. Population statistics
- D. Agricultural systems

**Answers:** B · B · B · C · C
