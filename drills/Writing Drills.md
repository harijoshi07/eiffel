## Task 1 — Build a Sentence

**Construction order:**
1. Find subject + finite verb
2. Decide: statement / direct question / embedded question
3. Place objects and complements
4. Attach modifiers next to what they describe
5. Check tense, agreement, articles, punctuation
6. Remove any distractor that duplicates a grammatical role

**Critical rule — embedded questions use statement word order:**
> ✗ She wants to know **where is the library**.
> ✓ She wants to know **where the library is**.

---

### Set A — Statements (10 items)

Arrange the words into a correct sentence. One item in each set may be a distractor.

1. `students / the / submitted / assignment / before / deadline / the`
2. `researchers / discovered / unexpected / an / result / during / the / experiment`
3. `the / city / council / approved / the / development / new / plan`
4. `professor / the / a / detailed / provided / explanation / of / the / theory`
5. `the / report / was / completed / ahead / of / schedule / by / the / team`
6. `students / who / attend / regularly / tend / to / perform / better`
7. `the / data / suggests / that / temperature / affects / plant / growth`
8. `a / large / amount / of / funding / was / allocated / to / the / project`
9. `the / library / closes / at / midnight / on / weekdays`
10. `the / committee / decided / to / postpone / the / event / until / spring`

**Answers:**
1. The students submitted the assignment before the deadline.
2. Researchers discovered an unexpected result during the experiment.
3. The city council approved the new development plan.
4. The professor provided a detailed explanation of the theory.
5. The report was completed ahead of schedule by the team.
6. Students who attend regularly tend to perform better.
7. The data suggests that temperature affects plant growth.
8. A large amount of funding was allocated to the project.
9. The library closes at midnight on weekdays.
10. The committee decided to postpone the event until spring.

## Task 2 — Write an Email

**Time: 7 minutes per email.** Use the workflow below.

```
0:00–1:00   Identify: audience · purpose · every required point
1:00–5:30   Write
5:30–7:00   Check: completeness · clarity · verbs · tone
```

**Checklist before submitting:**
- [ ] Purpose clear in the first two sentences
- [ ] Every prompt requirement answered
- [ ] Tone matches the recipient (formal for professor / semi-formal for classmate)
- [ ] Specific request stated
- [ ] Appropriate greeting and sign-off

---

### Email Prompt 1 — Request for Extension

> You are a university student. You need to email your professor to:
> - explain that you have been ill for the past week
> - request a one-week extension on your essay
> - offer to provide medical documentation

Write the email. (7 minutes)

**Model response:**

> Dear Professor Chen,
>
> I am writing to request an extension on the essay due this Friday. I have been unwell with a flu since Monday and have been unable to attend classes or complete significant work.
>
> I would be grateful if I could submit the essay one week later, by next Friday. I am happy to provide documentation from the university health centre if that would be helpful.
>
> Thank you for your understanding. I hope to be back in class by Wednesday.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your name]

**Checklist review:** Purpose ✓ · All three points addressed ✓ · Formal tone ✓ · Specific request ✓ · Appropriate sign-off ✓

### Email Prompt 2 — Change of Meeting

> You are a student group leader. You need to email your group members to:
> - explain that the Friday meeting needs to be rescheduled
> - propose Saturday at 2 p.m. as an alternative
> - ask them to confirm if the new time works

Write the email. (7 minutes)

**Model response:**

> Hi everyone,
>
> I wanted to let you know that our Friday meeting will need to be rescheduled. A room booking issue has made the original time slot unavailable.
>
> I'd like to propose Saturday at 2 p.m. as an alternative. We could use the group study room on the second floor of the library, which I can book now if this works for everyone.
>
> Please reply and let me know whether Saturday at 2 p.m. is convenient for you. I'll confirm the booking once I hear back.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

**Checklist review:** Purpose ✓ · All three points addressed ✓ · Appropriate tone ✓ · Clear request ✓ · Sign-off ✓

### Email Prompt 4 — Recommending a Restaurant

> A friend is visiting your city for the first time and has asked for a restaurant recommendation. Email your friend to:
> - recommend a specific restaurant and explain why you like it
> - describe what kind of food it serves
> - give practical information about how to get there or how to book

Write the email. (7 minutes)

**Model response:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'd recommend trying the Rosewood Café near the central station — it's one of my favourites in the city.
>
> It serves Mediterranean food, including excellent pasta and fresh salads. The prices are reasonable and the atmosphere is relaxed, which makes it perfect for a casual meal. I've been there many times and have never had a disappointing dish.
>
> It's about a five-minute walk from the main station exit — just head north along Station Road and you'll see it on the left. They do take reservations through their website, which I'd recommend for a Friday or Saturday evening.
>
> I hope you enjoy it!
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

## Task 3 — Academic Discussion

**Time: 10 minutes per response.** Workflow:

```
0:00–1:30   Choose position · reason · example
1:30–8:30   Write
8:30–10:00  Remove repetition · fix obvious errors
```

**Structure:**
1. **Position** — answer the question directly in the first sentence
2. **Reason** — explain the main logic
3. **Example** — make it concrete and specific
4. **Connection** — engage with another viewpoint briefly
5. **Implication** — state why this matters

**Self-score rubric (0–2 each):**

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task fulfillment | major parts missing | mostly complete | fully complete |
| Organisation | hard to follow | generally clear | controlled, cohesive |
| Development | unsupported | some support | specific, relevant support |
| Grammar | errors block meaning | noticeable but clear | strong control |
| Vocabulary | often inaccurate | adequate | precise and natural |

**Target:** 8 / 10 minimum. Rewrite anything below 8.

---

### Discussion Prompt 1 — Technology in Education

> **Professor:** Technology has changed how students learn. Some argue that digital tools such as laptops and smartphones distract students and reduce focus. Others believe technology helps students learn more effectively. What is your view? Give specific reasons and examples.
>
> **Student A (Maya):** I think technology is mostly a distraction. In my experience, having a phone nearby made it hard to concentrate on lectures because I kept checking notifications.
>
> **Student B (Leo):** I disagree. Digital tools let students access information instantly and collaborate with classmates across different locations. That flexibility is essential today.

Write your response. (10 minutes)

**Model response:**

> I believe technology genuinely improves learning when used purposefully, and the concern about distraction is a management issue rather than a reason to remove it entirely.
>
> The most significant advantage is access to resources. During a research project on climate policy last year, I was able to read primary sources, compare statistics from different governments, and view documentary footage within a single study session — none of which would have been possible with a textbook alone. This depth of access allowed me to write a much more nuanced argument than I otherwise could have.
>
> Maya raises a valid point about distraction, which I have also experienced. However, the solution is to develop better self-regulation habits — for example, putting a phone in silent mode during lectures — rather than eliminating digital tools altogether. Leo's point about collaboration is equally important: shared documents allow groups to work on projects without having to meet physically every time.
>
> Ultimately, the value of technology depends on how deliberately it is used. Teaching students how to use it effectively should be part of the curriculum.

**Word count check:** ~170 words ✓ · Position stated ✓ · Reason + example ✓ · Connection to both peers ✓ · Implication ✓

### Discussion Prompt 2 — Working Independently vs. in a Team

> **Professor:** In many careers today, the ability to work in a team is considered essential. However, some research suggests that individuals often produce their best work alone. Do you think people are more productive working independently or in a team? Explain your view with reasons and examples.
>
> **Student A (Priya):** I think teamwork is better because different people bring different strengths. A diverse team can solve problems that no single person could tackle alone.
>
> **Student B (James):** I find working alone more productive. When I work with others, I spend too much time coordinating and not enough time actually doing the work.

Write your response. (10 minutes)

**Model response:**

> I believe the most effective approach depends on the type of task, and that either method can be highly productive when matched appropriately.
>
> For creative or analytical work that requires sustained focus, working independently is often superior. When I was preparing for a national essay competition, I made significantly more progress by working in three-hour solo sessions than during the group study sessions I had tried earlier. Without interruptions or the need to explain my reasoning constantly, I could follow ideas to their conclusion.
>
> Priya is right, however, that certain problems genuinely require a range of expertise. A medical team diagnosing a rare condition benefits from specialists who each see different aspects of the same problem. James's concern about coordination costs is real, but it reflects poor team management rather than a flaw in teamwork itself.
>
> The most practical conclusion is that organisations should assign tasks based on their nature: independent work for deep thinking, collaborative work for problems that require multiple perspectives.
