# Listening Drills

Simulate real test conditions: play audio once, no replaying, note-take during listening.
Use the scripts below to practise when audio is unavailable — have someone read them to you, or use text-to-speech.

---

## Task 1 — Listen and Choose a Response

Read each prompt. Choose the most logical and socially natural reply.
Identify the speech act before choosing.

### Set A (15 items)

**Speech acts:** ask · invite · apologise · suggest · confirm · complain · refuse · warn · request · offer

1. "Could you send me the revised schedule by this afternoon?"
   - A. The meeting room is upstairs.
   - B. Sure, I'll email it right away.
   - C. No, the schedule was revised last week.
   - D. I attended the meeting.
   - **Answer: B** (accepts request + states action)

2. "I'm afraid I won't be able to attend your presentation tomorrow."
   - A. That's fine — I hope everything is okay.
   - B. The presentation starts at 2 p.m.
   - C. You should have registered earlier.
   - D. I didn't attend either.
   - **Answer: A** (polite acknowledgement of apology/notice)

3. "Have you submitted the assignment yet?"
   - A. Yes, the assignment was interesting.
   - B. Not yet — I'm planning to do it tonight.
   - C. The deadline is Thursday.
   - D. I submitted it last semester.
   - **Answer: B** (directly answers yes/no + gives status)

4. "Would you like me to help you carry those boxes?"
   - A. The boxes are very heavy.
   - B. They were delivered this morning.
   - C. That's very kind of you — thank you.
   - D. I don't need any boxes.
   - **Answer: C** (accepts the offer politely)

5. "The lab results still aren't ready."
   - A. I see. When do you expect them?
   - B. The lab is on the second floor.
   - C. Results are important for research.
   - D. That's great news.
   - **Answer: A** (natural follow-up to a status update)

6. "You really shouldn't wait until the last minute to register."
   - A. The registration office is closed.
   - B. You're right — I'll do it today.
   - C. I registered for two courses.
   - D. Last minute isn't that bad.
   - **Answer: B** (accepts the advice)

7. "Do you know where Professor Lia's office is?"
   - A. She teaches biology.
   - B. I think it's on the third floor, Room 312.
   - C. She is available on Tuesdays.
   - D. I don't know her very well.
   - **Answer: B** (answers the exact question)

8. "I'm sorry, but the café closes at 5 p.m. today."
   - A. I'll have a coffee, please.
   - B. Oh, that's earlier than usual. Thanks for letting me know.
   - C. The café is very popular.
   - D. I'll come back tomorrow.
   - **Answer: B** (acknowledges the information naturally)

9. "Why don't we meet at the library instead of the cafeteria?"
   - A. The cafeteria has good food.
   - B. The library is quite far from campus.
   - C. That sounds good — which floor?
   - D. I study alone.
   - **Answer: C** (agrees + asks a natural follow-up)

10. "I can't believe how loud it is in here."
    - A. I know — do you want to find a quieter spot?
    - B. The room was renovated last year.
    - C. Loud music is common on campus.
    - D. I'll turn down the volume.
    - **Answer: A** (empathises + offers a practical solution)

11. "The printer on the second floor is out of paper."
    - A. Thank you — I'll use the one downstairs.
    - B. I need to print five pages.
    - C. Printers should be checked regularly.
    - D. The second floor is the science wing.
    - **Answer: A** (acts on the information practically)

12. "Are you ready for the quiz tomorrow?"
    - A. The quiz is worth 20 percent.
    - B. Not really — I still have a few chapters to review.
    - C. I prefer written exams.
    - D. Tomorrow is Tuesday.
    - **Answer: B** (natural honest response)

13. "Could you explain that again? I didn't quite catch it."
    - A. Of course — I said the report is due Friday.
    - B. I explained it already.
    - C. You should listen more carefully.
    - D. The report is on the desk.
    - **Answer: A** (restates the information helpfully)

14. "I'm thinking of switching my major to economics."
    - A. Economics is a popular subject.
    - B. Really? What made you decide that?
    - C. You should take more courses.
    - D. The economics building is near the library.
    - **Answer: B** (shows genuine interest + invites elaboration)

15. "You left your umbrella in the lecture hall."
    - A. It rains a lot here.
    - B. Oh, thank you — I'll go get it now.
    - C. I usually carry an umbrella.
    - D. The lecture was very long.
    - **Answer: B** (responds naturally to useful information)

### Conversation 1

> **Student:** Hi, I'm here about the internship application. I submitted everything online, but I got an email saying my transcript is missing.
>
> **Advisor:** Yes, I see that. The system requires an official transcript — a PDF you download yourself isn't accepted. You need to request one through the Registrar's Office.
>
> **Student:** How long does that take?
>
> **Advisor:** Usually two to three business days. The application deadline is Friday, so if you request it today, you should be fine.
>
> **Student:** What if it doesn't arrive in time?
>
> **Advisor:** In that case, email me directly, and I can note that it's pending. That usually satisfies the committee if the rest of the application is complete.
>
> **Student:** Great — I'll go to the Registrar right now. Thank you.

**Q1.** What is the student's main problem?
- A. He missed the internship application deadline.
- B. His online transcript was not accepted.
- C. He does not know where the Registrar's Office is.
- D. His application was rejected by the committee.

**Q2.** What does the advisor suggest if the transcript arrives late?
- A. Resubmit the entire application after the deadline
- B. Request an extension from the committee
- C. Email the advisor directly to note the transcript is pending
- D. Submit a digital copy instead

**Q3.** What will the student most likely do next?
- A. Go to the Registrar's Office
- B. Email the internship committee
- C. Redownload his transcript PDF
- D. Wait two to three days before acting

**Answers:** B · C · A

### Conversation 2

> **Student:** Professor, I wanted to ask about my essay grade. I got a 3, and I'm not sure what I did wrong.
>
> **Professor:** Your argument was clear, but the main issue was your sources. You cited only one peer-reviewed article — the guidelines say a minimum of three.
>
> **Student:** Oh, I didn't realise the requirement was that strict.
>
> **Professor:** It's in the syllabus. That said, I do offer one revision per semester. If you add two more peer-reviewed sources and revise the analysis sections, I can reconsider the grade.
>
> **Student:** How long do I have?
>
> **Professor:** Two weeks from today. Submit the revised version through the course portal.

**Q1.** Why did the student receive a band 3?
- A. The argument was unclear and poorly structured
- B. The essay was submitted after the deadline
- C. Too few peer-reviewed sources were cited
- D. The writing style was informal

**Q2.** What does the professor offer the student?
- A. An automatic grade increase
- B. One opportunity to revise the essay this semester
- C. Extra time to submit the original essay
- D. A meeting to review every paragraph

**Q3.** What does the student need to do to improve the grade?
- A. Rewrite the entire essay
- B. Add three new sources and a new conclusion
- C. Add two peer-reviewed sources and revise the analysis
- D. Submit a reflection on the original grade

**Answers:** C · B · C

### Talk 1 — Biomimicry in Engineering

> Engineers have long drawn inspiration from the natural world. Biomimicry — the design of systems or materials based on biological structures — has produced some of the most innovative solutions in modern engineering.
>
> One well-known example is Velcro. Its inventor, George de Mestral, noticed in 1941 that burrs from a plant clung to his dog's fur. When he examined the burrs under a microscope, he found tiny hooks that caught on loops in the fur. He replicated this mechanism to create Velcro, which is now used in everything from clothing to spacecraft.
>
> Another application involves sharkskin. Shark scales have tiny riblets that reduce drag and resist bacteria. Engineers have modelled swimsuit materials and hospital surfaces on this structure. Studies show that riblet-based surfaces can reduce fluid drag by up to 10 percent.
>
> Critics of biomimicry point out that biological systems evolved under very different pressures than human engineering needs, and that translating a biological mechanism to a manufactured product is rarely straightforward. Nevertheless, researchers argue that the natural world's three-billion-year testing period offers an unmatched library of proven solutions.

**Q1.** What is the main topic of this talk?
- A. How animals protect themselves from predators
- B. The use of biological structures as models for engineering design
- C. The history of George de Mestral's inventions
- D. Problems with modern manufacturing techniques

**Q2.** Why does the speaker mention Velcro?
- A. To explain that Velcro is better than traditional fasteners
- B. As an example of biomimicry applied to a familiar product
- C. To contrast biological design with industrial design
- D. To support the critics' argument about translating mechanisms

**Q3.** What property of sharkskin are engineers trying to replicate?
- A. Its colour-changing ability
- B. Its ability to generate electricity
- C. Its drag reduction and antibacterial properties
- D. Its resistance to pressure at great depth

**Q4.** What is the critics' main concern about biomimicry?
- A. Biological systems are too complex to study
- B. Natural designs are protected by intellectual property law
- C. Translating biological mechanisms to manufacturing is difficult
- D. Biomimicry is too expensive for practical use

**Answers:** B · B · C · C

### Talk 2 — The Role of Sleep in Memory

> Most people think of sleep as passive rest. Research over the past two decades has shown that sleep plays an active and essential role in memory consolidation — the process by which newly acquired information is stabilised and integrated into long-term memory.
>
> During sleep, the brain replays the day's experiences. This replay occurs primarily during slow-wave sleep, the deep phase that dominates the first half of the night. The hippocampus, which temporarily stores new information, transfers it to the cortex for long-term storage during this replay process.
>
> Studies using targeted memory reactivation — playing soft sounds linked to previously learned objects while participants sleep — show that memories associated with those sounds are better recalled the next day. This suggests the replay is not random but can be guided.
>
> One implication for learning is that sleeping shortly after studying improves retention compared to staying awake and continuing to study. This has led some researchers to recommend short post-study naps as a practical study strategy, particularly for complex material.

**Q1.** What is the main argument of this talk?
- A. The hippocampus is damaged by sleep deprivation
- B. Sleep is an active process that consolidates memory
- C. Deep sleep occurs mainly in the second half of the night
- D. Studying while tired is ineffective

**Q2.** What happens during slow-wave sleep according to the speaker?
- A. The cortex sends information to the hippocampus
- B. The brain enters a state of complete inactivity
- C. New information is transferred from the hippocampus to the cortex
- D. Memory replays are triggered by sound cues

**Q3.** What do the targeted memory reactivation studies suggest?
- A. All memories are replayed equally during sleep
- B. External cues can guide which memories are replayed
- C. Only visual memories benefit from sleep consolidation
- D. Memory consolidation occurs mainly during REM sleep

**Q4.** What practical suggestion do some researchers make based on this research?
- A. Study for longer periods without breaks
- B. Avoid sleeping within four hours of studying
- C. Take a short nap after studying complex material
- D. Review material immediately upon waking

**Answers:** B · C · B · C
