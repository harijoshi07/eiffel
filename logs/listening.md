### Misses

**T2 M1 Q8** (Full-Length 2)
- Audio: Woman: "I'm not available this afternoon."
- Options: A. So am I. / B. It takes a while. / C. I didn't mean it. / **D. Well, I understand.**
- My answer: A. Key: D. Cause chosen: L5 Missed implied meaning.
- My reasoning (my words, shortened): I narrowed it to "so am I" and "I understand" and reasoned that I was replying to the same situation. I heard "not available" and answered "me neither", assuming I had been the one proposing a meeting.
- What went wrong: she gave a refusal. "So am I" assumes a shared situation that the sentence does not give. "Well, I understand" accepts her refusal. I invented a context (I had asked her to meet) to make A work.

**T6 M1 Q4** (Teacher Resources 4)
- Audio: Woman: "It's quite warm, isn't it?"
- Options: A. I haven't had a chance to check the schedule. / B. It's not in the budget for this year. / C. The hallway lights are out again. / **D. Especially for this time of year.**
- My answer: C. Key: D. Cause chosen: L5 Missed implied meaning.
- My reasoning (my words, shortened): I went through the options in order. C seemed to link lights to heat. I did not seriously check D. I now see "out again" does not mean warm, and D agrees with her remark. I spent 9s, my longest of the eight, and still missed.
- What went wrong: I built a link (lights give off heat) instead of asking what she wants from the reply. A tag question is an invitation to agree. C does not answer it. I also stopped before checking D.

### Pattern
- Both misses are responses to a **statement**, not a question. 21 of the 112 prompts are statements, so I missed 2 of 21 statements and 0 of 91 questions. Small sample, but it is the only split I found.
- Both times I made up a situation to justify a wrong option ("I asked her to meet", "lights make heat"). Both times the right option was the plain, natural reply.
- Both times I did not fully evaluate the option that turned out right.
- Decision time does not explain it: the T6 Q4 miss came after my longest decision.
