## Lock-in check (every Academic and Daily Life question, 15 seconds)
1. **Task:** say what is being asked in five words ("why mention Pont du Gard", "how many *with* Ms. Green").
2. **Evidence:** point to one sentence. No sentence means no answer.
3. **Echo test:** if your option contains a word from the passage, does the word play the same role there?
4. **Literal kill:** knock out each wrong option with a false claim you can quote ("P2 mentions no challenges", "no technique is described").

If 2 or 3 fails, the option is a decoy even if it feels right.

## By question type

### Purpose ("why does the author mention X")
- Find the sentence. The answer is the claim attached to X, usually after a comma, "like", or "such as".
- Test case: "Some Roman aqueducts, like the Pont du Gard in France, **are still standing today**, showcasing…" so the claim is *still standing*, which is **preserved**. "Describe techniques" and "how aqueducts were constructed" need the sentence to explain how, and it doesn't.
- Daily Life version: the form title or the opening sentence states the purpose. A field label (card number) does not.

### Paragraph relationship
- Before reading the options, write each paragraph's job in six words or fewer. TR3 M2: **P2 = examples of the era's shows. P3 = challenges and legacy.**
- Test each option literally:
  - "Expands on X" requires X to be in the earlier paragraph. P2 has no challenges, so A is out.
  - "Contradicts" requires a statement of the opposite. P3 ends "Nonetheless… indelible mark", so D is out.
  - "New issue not covered" fits, because P2's "issues" were what a show tackled.
- Watch for the same word with a different referent.

### Detail ("what is one benefit of X over Y")
- Go to the sentence containing both X and the contrast. Choose the option built from the words of the stated result.
- TR5 M2: "Because oil paints dry more slowly… artists could use **blending and layering** to create more texture and richer colors." So the benefit is **layers**. "Richer" is not "realistic", and "more realistic" is from paragraph 1, about Renaissance paintings overall.

### Insert sentence (2 of 4 missed, so this is the priority)
1. **Circle the anchors** in the sentence: pronouns (This, These, Such), connectors (But, However, Also), and repeated words.
2. **Predict what must come before it.** A pronoun needs a noun or event; "But" needs something to contrast.
3. **Predict what must come after it.** A question needs its answer next.
4. **Test each position** by reading the sentence before, the insert, and the sentence after. Reject any position where the next sentence now makes no sense.

Worked examples:
- **TR5 M1 (key A):** "This is all the more remarkable considering the lack of technology at the time." "Remarkable" echoes S1 ("navigated… with remarkable precision… during the medieval period"), and "at the time" echoes "medieval". At C it would follow a description of the sunstone tool, which is technology, and would come just before "However, evidence… remains scarce".
- **TR2 M2 (key C):** "But what affects birds' sleep norms?" It needs a bird statement before it, and the answer must follow. C sits after the last bird sentence, and the next sentence is "Sleep patterns have evolved based on environmental needs", where "sleep norms" echoes "sleep patterns". Position C is at the start of paragraph 3, and a paragraph start is a legal position.

### Complete the Words (word form)
1. Count the letters.
2. Write the **whole word family** (discover, discovers, discovered, discovering, discovery, discoveries).
3. Filter by slot: after *the/a* a noun; in a list a plural; opening a sentence with an action, -ing; after *to/can/may* a base verb.
4. Filter by meaning, then check the next word.

Mark each miss as *not generated* (F1) or *generated but misjudged* in the log.
